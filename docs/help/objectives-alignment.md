# Objectives & alignment

> Learning objectives are the spine of good design — they say what a learner should be able to*do*, and everything else (content, assessment) should line up behind them. Tavoro makes that alignment a first-class, checkable thing instead of a spreadsheet you keep in your head.

## What makes a good objective

A strong objective is **observable and measurable** — it uses a verb you can actually see or score. “Understand phishing” isn’t testable; “identify three signs of a phishing email” is. Tavoro tags each objective with a level from **Bloom’s Revised Taxonomy** and suggests strong verbs for that level, to nudge you away from fuzzy ones like “know” or “understand.”

**Remember** — Recall facts — define, list, identify, name.

**Understand** — Explain ideas — summarize, describe, classify, compare.

**Apply** — Use it in a new situation — demonstrate, solve, execute.

**Analyze** — Draw connections — differentiate, organize, diagnose.

**Evaluate** — Justify a stand — critique, assess, recommend.

**Create** — Produce something new — design, construct, develop.

## The alignment spine

Once objectives exist, two things attach to them — and Tavoro shows you the gaps:

- **Content teaches them.** On any storyboard screen, tag the objectives that screen teaches. Each objective then shows how many screens cover it (and warns if it’s **Not yet taught**).
- **Assessment measures them.** Add assessment items to the project and link each to the objectives it checks. Each objective shows how many items assess it (and warns if it’s **Not yet assessed**).

> **Note.** The assessment side is a lightweight **question bank**, not an exam engine — Tavoro tracks what you’re measuring and how it aligns, while the actual quiz lives in your authoring tool or LMS.

## How to use it

![The Learning objectives & alignment section: each objective with its Bloom level and how many screens teach it and items assess it](../../public/help-shots/alignment.png)
*Each objective shows its Bloom level and its taught and assessed coverage.*

1. Open a project and find **Learning objectives & alignment**. Add objectives — one per thing a learner should be able to do — and set each one’s Bloom level.
2. In the project’s storyboards, expand a screen and use the **Teaches** chips (O1, O2, …) to mark which objectives that screen covers.
3. Back on the project, add **assessment items** and toggle which objectives each one **Assesses:**. Reuse a question with **Add from the question bank**, or share a good one with **Save to bank** (see [Question bank](question-bank.md)).
4. Read the coverage summary at the top: any objective flagged **Not yet taught** or **Not yet assessed** — and any screens that teach nothing — are your gaps to close.

### Why it’s worth it

This is the check that catches the expensive mistakes early: an objective nobody assesses, a screen that doesn’t map to any objective, an assessment that tests something you never taught. Fixing those on the alignment view is a lot cheaper than finding them in review.

> **Tip.** Objectives live on the project and drive its phases too — pair this with the [ADDIE & SAM](addie-sam.md) flow: write objectives in Analyze/Preparation, teach them in Design/Develop, and prove them in Evaluate.
